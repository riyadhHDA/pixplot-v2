An atlas of 80,716 artworks, on a single computer

PixPlot arranges large collections of artworks by visual similarity and helps you look in them for reworkings, motifs, gestures and catalogue errors. It suggests leads; the art historian checks them.

The questions you can ask it

Start from a folder of images, a museum IIIF collection or a dataset like WikiArt, with or without metadata. Each plate shows what the Atlas does, with a real example from WikiArt 81k. A computed match is still a lead: it proves neither influence nor attribution.

Neighbours and "Why?"

Who copied, quoted or reworked whom?

For each work, PixPlot gives the nearest neighbours in four regards. The comparator then shows which zones of one answer which zones of the other.

On WikiArt
for the Sower Van Gogh copied after Millet in 1889, the 1st neighbour is his 1881 copy, the 2nd Millet's original; four zones answer each other.
In the Atlas
click a work, then C to compare

Detail and zone search

Where does this motif come back?

Draw a box round a hand, a drapery or an object, or describe the detail in words: PixPlot searches 1.6 million zones and boxes the zone it found in every work.

Tried
"a skull": El Greco, Cézanne, Zurbarán, Dürer, Mabuse, Ribera
In the Atlas
"Search this detail" on the record, or /

Free-text search

Which works deal with this theme?

Type a sentence, in English or French. SigLIP 2 ranks the whole collection by how close each work is to it, including works with no title or label.

Tried
"Annunciation in a garden"
Measure
over 10 English sentences, 96 of the first 100 results are relevant (ARCHITECTURE § 31)

"Posture" regard, on trial

Does the same gesture come back from one century to the next?

PixPlot detects figures, estimates their skeleton and compares poses, mirrored ones included. It finds gestural formulas, Aby Warburg's Pathosformeln, that the other regards miss.

On WikiArt
a Venus by Titian and a sleeping apostle in Mantegna's Agony in the Garden, mirrored (similarity 0.93)
Review
25 "same gesture" pairs validated out of 37 proposed

A similar pose is a lead, not proof that one artist saw the other. See the real plate

To check, and duplicates

Is my catalogue reliable?

When all the neighbours of a work contradict its label, it goes to a list of works to check. It holds catalogue errors, and also truly singular works. PixPlot also finds works entered twice.

On WikiArt
a Sisley of 1894 whose ten labelled neighbours are all Monets; the same Corot entered twice, as "Romanticism" and "Realism" (839 duplicates, 702 under two styles)
In the Atlas
V to check

Four regards, disagreements

What brings these two works together?

Form, subject, colour and touch stay separate, never merged into one score. When two works are close in one regard and far in another, the gap says what they share: a layout reused for another theme, the same subject by two hands.

On WikiArt
an engraving after Rembrandt and a Bryullov: 3rd neighbour in form, but at the 17th percentile in subject
In the Atlas
D disagreements · I card of each regard (data, biases)

Filters, groups, dates, places

What is my collection made of?

The map groups the works and names the groups with art-historical vocabulary and Iconclass. Combined filters cross style, genre, artist, group, date and atypicality, showing what each choice would give. Timeline and geographic views, and a report of what is missing.

On WikiArt
"Baroque" + "portrait": 980 works lit on the map in 14 ms
In the Atlas
F filters · G groups

Learned notions

What does the model see that the catalogue does not name?

An autoencoder breaks the "subject" and "form" regards into about 2,000 notions each: objects, motifs, layouts. Each notion gets an Iconclass name when that name is reliable, and its works light up on the map.

On WikiArt
notion no. 607, named "ladder", gathers Depositions (Fra Angelico, Rosso, Rembrandt, Tintoretto); other notions: pumpkins, stripes, harvests
In the Atlas
O notions

Conceptual axes

Where does a work sit between two notions?

The whole collection is arranged between two words or two sets of sentences: sacred and profane, or Wölfflin's linear / painterly pair. What leaves the diagonal sheds light on the collection, and on how the model understands the words.

Measure
before 1800, Wölfflin's axis follows time, with a switch around 1550 (ARCHITECTURE § 43)

Notebook, Research Pack, assistant

How do I keep track of my research?

Leads go to the research notebook. The Research Pack export (.zip) gathers images, sourced metadata, scores, method and a datasheet of the collection. An assistant connected through MCP can search and compare with you and write in the same notebook.

Who does what

A vision model relates images through their pixels. It knows nothing of a commission, a workshop or a real line of descent, so each step stays separate.

  • PixPlotcomputes neighbourhoods, indexes zones and builds the maps.
  • The assistant (MCP)queries the collection, builds plates and suggests leads.
  • The art historianlooks at the works, checks the sources and decides what holds.

The Atlas as it is

All of WikiArt (80,716 works, 33.7 GB) processed end to end in about 1 h 21 on a desktop PC (Windows 11, Radeon RX 7900 GRE). The Atlas opens in a few seconds; a text or detail search answers in 0.25 s.

What each regard finds

Rule followed: a model enters PixPlot only if it improves a benchmark, and negative results are published. Eight models were measured and set aside, among them TIPSv2, PE-Spatial, SSCD, Depth Anything, PE-Core and CSD.

Share of the 10 nearest neighbours carrying the same label as the starting work, over 80,716 works
RegardartiststylegenrePointillismFauvism
Form (DINOv3)33.9 %52.4 %76.8 %21 %20 %
Subject (SigLIP 2)42.9 %62.0 %79.5 %44 %26 %
Touch (DINOv3, layer 6)35.0 %49.6 %62.8 %62 %30 %

SigLIP 2 best recovers the usual categories (artist, style, genre), largely because it learned art history's vocabulary from captions on the web.

DINOv3's intermediate layer catches up on styles defined by handling: Pointillism goes from 21 % to 62 %, and the artist's hand stands out better than with the global vector.

Direct copies come first (Raphael after Perugino, Van Gogh after Millet), but as soon as the artist transforms the model strongly (Picasso after El Greco), no regard follows yet.

WikiArt's metadata is noisy: the list of works to check brings up real misclassifications, such as religious scenes filed as genre scenes.

"Artist" column measured on 1,090 artists, after recovering the artist of 35,185 anonymous works with MMArt. Details and confidence intervals: ARCHITECTURE.md.

Install and try

You need Python 3.12. A GPU speeds up extraction a lot (AMD ROCm on Windows, or CUDA), but everything also runs on the CPU for a small folder. Nothing is sent to a remote server. See INSTALL_GPU.md.

# install
python -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\activate                 # Linux / macOS: source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e ".[dev,ml]"

# process a folder of images
pixplot ingest "D:/MyImages"           # catalogue, thumbnails, duplicates
pixplot import-metadata meta.csv       # artist, date, style… ("path" column required)
pixplot embed                          # DINOv3 vectors (GPU), resumable
pixplot analyze                        # groups, UMAP map, neighbourhoods
pixplot serve --open                   # opens the Atlas at http://127.0.0.1:8765

All commands: COMMANDS.md.
